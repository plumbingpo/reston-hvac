Most AC breakdowns in Reston trace back to a small part: the run capacitor. It helps start the compressor and condenser fan, loses strength slowly over the years, and tends to give out on the hottest afternoon, which is why so many service calls begin with an outdoor unit that hums while the fan sits motionless. Other frequent culprits include contactors with pitted, burned points, fan motors whose bearings have run dry, slow refrigerant leaks that steadily rob capacity, and evaporator coils that turn into blocks of ice once a dirty filter chokes the airflow.

At Reston HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because one warm-air complaint can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Central air, heat pumps running in cooling mode, and ductless mini splits from every major brand are all part of our daily work. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, most AC repairs are completed the same visit. Signs You Need AC Repair in Reston

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Bubbling or hissing noises that point to a possible refrigerant leak
  • Cooling bills climbing while the house stays sticky and warm
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below
  • Clicking or humming outside with no fan movement
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • The system short-cycling every few minutes without cooling the house

Why Hire a Professional for AC Repair?

An air conditioner packs several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks throws money away while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Replace the filter every month in summer, since restricted airflow is the top cause of frozen coils
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
